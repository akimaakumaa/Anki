BACK: [type: conversational idiom]
Forms: break /breɪk/ — broke /brəʊk/ — broken /ˈbrəʊkən/
**broke the ice** = To do or say something to relieve tension or get conversation started in a strained or unfamiliar situation. Used when meeting new people or starting a difficult meeting.

Example 1: I bought her a coffee to try and break the ice after our argument.
Example 2: He broke the ice by bringing his puppy to the team picnic.

Watch out: Usually used with "the" (broke *the* ice), not "some ice" or "an ice". 
Similar phrase: got things going, eased the tension

**chose the worst time** = To decide to do something at the most inconvenient or inappropriate moment possible. Used to express frustration about someone else's timing, or to apologize for interrupting.

Example 1: I know I chose the worst time to ask, but can I borrow fifty bucks?
Example 2: They chose the worst time to announce the budget cuts.

Watch out: Often paired with "couldn't have" for extra emphasis (e.g., "You couldn't have chosen a worse time"). 
Similar phrase: picked a bad moment, bad timing

BACK: [type: phrasal verb]
Forms: freeze /friːz/ — froze /frəʊz/ — frozen /ˈfrəʊzən/
**froze up** = To suddenly become completely unable to move, speak, or think clearly, usually due to panic, fear, or extreme nervousness.

Example 1: He totally froze up when he saw his ex-girlfriend walk into the cafe.
Example 2: I tried to answer the officer, but my mind just went blank and I froze up.

Watch out: Don't confuse it with physically feeling cold. In conversation, this almost strictly refers to mental or physical paralysis from anxiety.
Similar phrase: blanked, clammed up

BACK: [type: conversational idiom]
Forms: speak /spiːk/ — spoke /spəʊk/ — spoken /ˈspəʊkən/
**spoke too soon** = To say something that quickly turns out to be wrong, usually because you assumed a good outcome before it was guaranteed. Used to admit a mistake in judgment or prediction.

Example 1: I was just bragging about never getting sick, but I spoke too soon—I have a fever today.
Example 2: Looks like we spoke too soon about the traffic being light; there's a huge jam ahead.

Watch out: Almost always used in the past simple (V2) because you are referring to the immediate past when you made the incorrect statement.
Similar phrase: celebrated too early, jinxed it

Example 1: The bride looked beautiful, but her adorable flower girl completely stole the show.

**woke up to** = To experience or discover a specific (often surprising or negative) situation immediately upon waking from sleep. 

Example 1: I woke up to the smell of fresh coffee and pancakes this morning.
