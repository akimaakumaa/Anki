FRONT:

1. I was ___ and raised in Chicago, so I'm used to freezing winters.
2. Were you ___ and raised here, or did you move from somewhere else?
3. We were both ___ and raised in a tiny town before moving to London.

BACK: [type: conversational phrase]
Forms: bear /bɛə/ — bore /bɔː/ — born /bɔːn/
**born and raised** = used to specify the geographic location where you were born and spent your entire childhood growing up.

Example 1: I was born and raised in California, so I love the beach.
Example 2: We were born and raised in the same neighborhood.

Watch out: Make sure to always use "and raised", never "or raised". Also, do not forget the auxiliary verb "was/were".
Similar phrase: originally from, grew up in

FRONT:

1. I could have ___ I left my car keys right here on the table!
2. Could you have ___ she told us to meet her at 6:00 PM instead?
3. We could have ___ the store was open today, but the doors are locked.

BACK: [type: spoken idiom]
Forms: swear /swɛə/ — swore /swɔː/ — sworn /swɔːn/
**could have sworn** = used when you feel absolutely certain about a past event or fact, only to discover that you were mistaken.

Example 1: I could have sworn I turned off the oven before we left the house.
Example 2: You could have sworn you saw him walk into the office earlier.

Watch out: Avoid using the past simple form "swore" in this specific phrase; it must always be the past participle "sworn".
Similar phrase: was positive that, was certain that

FRONT:

1. I'm completely ___ between buying the laptop or saving the money.
2. Are you ___ between accepting the new job or staying here?
3. We're really ___ between going out to dinner or ordering takeout.

BACK: [type: conversational phrase]
Forms: tear /tɛə/ — tore /tɔː/ — torn /tɔːn/
**torn between** = used when you are having a very hard time choosing between two appealing options or paths.

Example 1: I am torn between visiting my family or going on a beach trip.
Example 2: You look totally torn between those two dresses.

Watch out: Ensure you use the past participle "torn", not the past simple "tore", when describing this state of indecision.
Similar phrase: split between, on the fence about

FRONT:

1. I am absolutely ___ out after that intense workout at the gym.
2. You look completely ___ out; you should probably go straight to bed.
3. We were totally ___ out by the time we reached the mountain top.

BACK: [type: phrasal verb]
Forms: wear /wɛə/ — wore /wɔː/ — worn /wɔːn/
**worn out** = used when you are utterly exhausted, drained, or have completely run out of physical or mental energy.

Example 1: I am too worn out to study any more vocabulary tonight.
Example 2: We were so worn out from traveling all day that we fell asleep instantly.

Watch out: Be careful with pronunciation: the vowel sound in "worn" is /ɔː/, not /ɜː/ (like in the word "turn").
Similar phrase: exhausted, dead tired
