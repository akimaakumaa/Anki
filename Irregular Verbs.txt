FRONT:
1. You shouldn't ___ your ___ to study abroad; it's an incredible program.
2. He completely ___ his ___ with the new client by arriving an hour late to the meeting.
3. I feel like we've ___ our ___ at getting cheap concert tickets since they sell out so fast.
Synonyms: miss an opportunity, waste a chance, mess up

BACK: [type: verb+noun]
**blow a chance** = miss or waste a good opportunity by making a mistake
Example 1: If you don't prepare for the interview, you will blow your chance.
Example 2: They blew their chance of winning the championship in the final minute of the game.
Watch out: Don't confuse this with "lose a chance"; "blow" implies that you personally made a mistake that caused the failure.

FRONT:
1. Try to sneak in quietly; we don't want to ___ any ___ to ourselves.
2. Her highly unusual outfit definitely ___ a lot of ___ at the formal conference.
3. The recent public protests have ___ widespread ___ to the housing crisis.
Synonyms: attract notice, catch the eye, turn heads

BACK: [type: verb+noun]
**draw attention** = make people look at or notice someone or something
Example 1: The loud argument in the restaurant drew everyone's attention.
Example 2: I've highlighted this text in red to draw your attention to the most important rule.
Watch out: We almost always use this with the preposition "to" when specifying the target: "draw attention TO something/someone."

FRONT:
1. I can't believe how fast the weekend ___ ___!
2. The deadline is approaching so fast because the last few weeks have just ___ ___.
3. Time really ___ ___ when you're busy working on an engaging project.
Synonyms: pass quickly, go fast, slip away

BACK: [type: chunk]
**fly by** = pass very quickly (almost always referring to time)
Example 1: The summer holidays always fly by so fast when you are a kid.
Example 2: We were having such a good conversation that the afternoon just flew by.
Watch out: This is typically used with the phrase "time flies" or when talking about periods of time like days, weeks, or years.

FRONT:
1. It takes a lot of hard work and financial investment to ___ a successful ___.
2. She ___ her small local ___ into a massive international tech company.
3. My cousin is trying to ___ his online ___, but he's currently struggling to find enough customers.
Synonyms: expand a company, build a business, scale up

BACK: [type: verb+noun]
**grow a business** = make a company bigger, more profitable, and successful
Example 1: We are currently looking for new investors to help grow our business.
Example 2: He wants to grow his business for five more years before he finally sells it.
Watch out: "Business" is often an uncountable noun (e.g., "we do business together"), but in this collocation, it refers to a specific company, making it countable ("a business").

FRONT:
1. I need to ___ this speech by ___ for the big presentation tomorrow.
2. She ___ the lyrics by ___ after listening to the pop song just twice.
3. All the actors have ___ their lines by ___ since last week's rehearsal.
Synonyms: have memorized, know word-for-word, remember perfectly

BACK: [type: chunk]
**know by heart** = have something memorized perfectly so you don't need to read it
Example 1: I've watched that movie so many times that I know the script by heart.
Example 2: You don't need to know the entire employee manual by heart, just the basic safety rules.
Watch out: This is often confused with "learn by heart". You first *learn* something by heart (the process), and then you *know* it by heart (the result).

FRONT:
1. We should ___ a huge ___ for his university graduation next month.
2. The marketing company ___ a lavish ___ to celebrate their record profits this year.
3. She has never ___ a ___ before, so she's worried that her guests won't have fun.
Synonyms: host a party, organize a celebration, have a gathering

BACK: [type: verb+noun]
**throw a party** = organize, host, and provide the space for a social gathering or celebration
Example 1: My roommates decided to throw a party on Friday night to celebrate the end of exams.
Example 2: If we manage to finish this project on time, I'm throwing a party!
Watch out: Many learners translate directly from their native language and say "make a party" or "do a party", but these are incorrect in English. Always use "throw", "host", or "have".
