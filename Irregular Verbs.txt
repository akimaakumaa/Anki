FRONT:

How do we usually ___ the ___ of onboarding a new employee?

Who ___ the ___ of reorganizing the company files yesterday?

Have you already ___ the ___ of applying for your travel visas?

BACK: [type: verb+noun]
begin the process = to start a series of actions needed to achieve a result
Example 1: We need to begin the process of moving to a new office next month.
Example 2: The hospital has begun the process of updating all patient records.
Watch out: Don't confuse "process" with "procedure" (a set of strict, official rules).
Synonyms: start the procedure, initiate the steps

FRONT:

Shall we ___ a ___ to the success of our new business partnership?

Who ___ a ___ to Sarah at her farewell dinner?

Have you ever ___ a ___ with champagne before?

BACK: [type: verb+noun]
drink a toast = to raise your glass and drink together in honor of someone or something
Example 1: I'd like to drink a toast to our wonderful hosts tonight.
Example 2: At the wedding gala, everyone drank a toast to the bride and groom.
Watch out: We say "drink a toast TO someone", not "FOR someone".
Synonyms: raise a glass, make a toast

FRONT:

What exact detail ___ a ___ when you listened to his story?

Has his explanation ___ a ___ with anyone on the jury?

BACK: [type: chunk]
ring a bell = to sound familiar, even if you cannot remember all the details
Example 1: That book title rings a bell, but I haven't actually read it.
Example 2: I saw him in the meeting, but his voice didn't ring a bell.
Watch out: This expression is frequently used in the negative (doesn't ring a bell).
Synonyms: sound familiar, spark a memory

Example 1: Be careful with that dress, or it might shrink in the wash.

Watch out: "Wash" here is used as a noun referring to the routine process of washing clothes.

FRONT:

BACK: [type: chunk]
sing along = to join in singing a piece of music that is already playing
Example 1: The band played their biggest hit and the whole crowd started to sing along.
Example 2: I absolutely love to sing along to the radio while I'm driving to work.
Watch out: We use the preposition "to" when mentioning the music (sing along to the song).

FRONT:

Does this heavy material normally ___ to the ___ of the tank?

What heavy object ___ to the ___ of the lake during the storm?

Have all the ancient coins already ___ to the ___ of the wishing well?

BACK: [type: chunk]
sink to the bottom = to drop downwards in water or liquid until reaching the lowest point
Example 1: If you drop a solid stone in the lake, it will sink to the bottom immediately.
Example 2: We watched the broken wooden chest sink to the bottom of the sea.
Watch out: Use "to" for the destination (sink TO the bottom), not "at" or "in".
Synonyms: go under, descend completely

FRONT:

Does any clear solution ___ to ___ right now?

What specific memory ___ to ___ when you smelled that perfume?

Has a better idea ___ to ___ since our last team meeting?

BACK: [type: chunk]
spring to mind = to suddenly or quickly appear in your thoughts
Example 1: When I think of great comedy movies, several classics immediately spring to mind.
Example 2: I asked for suggestions, but absolutely nothing sprang to mind right away.
Watch out: We say "spring TO mind", not "in mind" or "to my mind" for this exact expression.
Synonyms: come to mind, occur to someone

FRONT:

Is it dangerous to ___ against the ___ in this fast-moving river?

Who bravely ___ against the ___ to rescue the stranded boat?

Have you ever ___ against a strong ocean ___ during a severe storm?

BACK: [type: chunk]
swim against the current = to move through water in the opposite direction to its natural flow
Example 1: The rescue team had to swim against the current to reach the isolated survivors.
Example 2: It requires an enormous amount of physical strength to swim against the current in the ocean.
Watch out: Don't confuse "current" (the directional flow of water) with "currently" (an adverb meaning right now).
Synonyms: swim upstream, battle the tide
