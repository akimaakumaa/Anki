FRONT:
1. The magazine decided not to <b>publish</b> the ___ because the topic was too controversial.
2. Our professor wants to <b>publish</b> an ___ about climate change in a major science journal.
3. Did you manage to <b>publish</b> that ___ on your blog yesterday?

BACK: [type: verb+noun]
**publish an article** = to print a written piece in a magazine, newspaper, or online
Example 1: She hopes to publish an article about her travel experiences in Japan soon.
Example 2: The university requires its researchers to publish an article at least once a year.
Watch out: We say an article *on* or *about* a topic, not *of* a topic.
Synonyms: release a post, print a story, issue a paper

FRONT:
1. Could you <b>go</b> ___ and check if the mail has arrived yet?
2. The children usually <b>go</b> ___ to play in the living room after dinner.
3. I need to <b>go</b> ___ to the hotel reception desk to get a new room key.

BACK: [type: chunk]
**go downstairs** = to move to a lower floor in a building
Example 1: Let's go downstairs and see what Mom is cooking for lunch.
Example 2: If the fire alarm rings, you must go downstairs immediately using the stairs.
Watch out: Never say "go *to* downstairs." The word downstairs already includes the direction.
Synonyms: head downstairs, walk down

FRONT:
1. The doctor will see you shortly; <b>in the</b> ___, please fill out these medical forms.
2. The cake takes an hour to bake, so <b>in the</b> ___ we can wash the dishes.
3. My car is being repaired at the garage, so I'm using my bicycle <b>in the</b> ___.

BACK: [type: chunk]
**in the meantime** = during the time before something else happens
Example 1: I’ll check the flight prices; in the meantime, you should look for a cheap hotel.
Example 2: The boss is running late, so we should start the meeting in the meantime.
Watch out: Don't forget the word "the". It is always "in the meantime", never "in meantime".
Synonyms: meanwhile, for now, in the interim

FRONT:
1. You have to <b>wear</b> a dark ___ and a white blouse as part of the school uniform.
2. It was so hot that she decided to <b>wear</b> a ___ instead of her heavy jeans.
3. Are you going to <b>wear</b> a ___ to the job interview, or a pair of smart trousers?

BACK: [type: verb+noun]
**wear a skirt** = to have a piece of clothing that hangs from the waist over your legs
Example 1: I rarely wear a skirt in the winter because the weather gets too cold.
Example 2: Female employees were historically forced to wear a skirt in many corporate offices.
Watch out: A skirt only covers the bottom half of the body; a dress covers both the top and bottom.
Synonyms: put on a skirt, have a skirt on

FRONT:
1. The police asked the witness to come to the station and <b>make</b> a ___ about the accident.
2. The company CEO will <b>make</b> a public ___ about the recent data breach tomorrow morning.
3. I don't want to <b>make</b> a ___ to the press until I have spoken privately with my lawyer.

BACK: [type: verb+noun]
**make a statement** = to give an official account of facts or opinions, often publicly
Example 1: The Prime Minister is expected to make a statement on the economy later today.
Example 2: You will need to make a statement explaining exactly what you saw that night.
Watch out: Use "make" or "issue" a statement, never "do a statement" or "say a statement".
Synonyms: issue a statement, give a formal account

FRONT:
1. "I promise I'll pay you back tomorrow." "___, <b>right</b>. You still owe me from last month!"
2. "My dad is going to buy me a sports car for my birthday." "___, <b>right</b>, and I'm the Queen of England."
3. "The manager said we might get to leave work early today." "___, <b>right</b>, like that has ever happened before."

BACK: [type: chunk]
**yeah, right** = an informal, sarcastic expression used to show you don't believe what someone just said
Example 1: "I'm going to start going to the gym every single day at 5 AM." "Yeah, right."
Example 2: "He told me he completely fixed the Wi-Fi." "Yeah, right, the connection is still broken."
Watch out: The tone of voice is essential; it must be said sarcastically, or people will mistakenly think you agree with them.
Synonyms: sure, I don't believe you, nonsense

FRONT:
1. Even as a young child, she showed a <b>natural</b> ___ for playing the piano.
2. It takes years of intense practice to be a professional athlete; it's not always just a <b>natural</b> ___.
3. He has a <b>natural</b> ___ to make people feel calm and comfortable in stressful situations.

BACK: [type: adj+noun]
**natural ability** = a skill or talent that you are born with, without needing much training
Example 1: Dogs have a natural ability to sense when their owners are feeling sad or anxious.
Example 2: Despite his natural ability in mathematics, he chose to study history at university.
Watch out: We usually say "ability *to* do something," not "ability *of* doing something."
Synonyms: innate talent, natural skill, gift

FRONT:
1. The music at the rock concert was so loud that we were <b>barely</b> ___ to hear each other speak.
2. I injured my knee playing football yesterday, and now I'm <b>barely</b> ___ to walk up the stairs.
3. She was laughing so hard at the joke that she was <b>barely</b> ___ to breathe.

BACK: [type: chunk]
**barely able to** = having only just enough strength or capacity to do something; almost failing
Example 1: He was so exhausted after the night shift that he was barely able to keep his eyes open.
Example 2: The old, broken-down car was barely able to make it up the steep mountain hill.
Watch out: Remember that "can" has no future or perfect forms. You must use "will be able to" or "have been able to."
Synonyms: hardly able to, just capable of

FRONT:
1. We saved a lot of money on our holiday by flying with a <b>budget</b> ___ to Spain.
2. You usually have to pay extra fees for your heavy luggage on a <b>budget</b> ___.
3. Despite being a <b>budget</b> ___, the cabin crew's customer service was surprisingly excellent.

BACK: [type: adj+noun]
**budget airline** = a company that offers cheap flights but charges extra for food, drinks, and bags
Example 1: Flying with a budget airline is a perfect option if you only have a small backpack for the weekend.
Example 2: The new budget airline will start operating cheap flights across Europe next month.
Watch out: Don't confuse "airline" (the business/company) with "airplane" or "aircraft" (the flying vehicle).
Synonyms: low-cost carrier, cheap airline, discount airline

FRONT:
1. I spent an hour looking everywhere for my house keys, but they were in my jacket pocket <b>all</b> ___.
2. He pretended to be completely surprised by the birthday party, but I think he knew about it <b>all</b> ___.
3. The clever detective realized that the trusted butler was the art thief <b>all</b> ___.

BACK: [type: chunk]
**all along** = from the very beginning of a situation
Example 1: I knew all along that this complex project was going to take much longer than expected.
Example 2: She wasn't ignoring your texts; her phone was completely broken all along.
Watch out: Don't confuse "along" (meaning with/forward) with "alone" (meaning by yourself).
Synonyms: from the start, the whole time, right from the beginning
