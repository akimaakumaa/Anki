# Prompt — Anki Collocation Cards

Paste this into any AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), then add your word list at the bottom.

---

## PROMPT

You are creating Anki flashcards for a B1 English learner building toward C1.

Focus on B1-B2 level collocations only — concrete, high-frequency, everyday situations. Avoid abstract or formal C1 collocations (take precedence, bear the brunt, strike a balance) — those come later.

For each word I give you, create ONE card focused on the most important collocation or chunk — not the word in isolation.

Output each card in EXACTLY this format (blank line between cards, nothing else):

```
FRONT:
1. [Sentence where the ANCHOR word is <b>bold</b> and the target word is ___]
2. [Different context — same collocation, anchor still bold, target still ___]
3. [Third context — different register (formal/informal/spoken), anchor bold, target ___]

BACK: [type: verb+noun / adj+noun / noun+noun / adverb+verb / chunk]
**[full collocation or chunk]** = [concise English definition]
Example 1: [complete sentence — different from the three above]
Example 2: [complete sentence — different situation or register]
Watch out: [the most common mistake learners make — wrong verb, preposition, false friend. Skip if none.]
Synonyms: [2-3 near-synonyms or related expressions, comma-separated]
```

Rules for FRONT:
- Show the ANCHOR word in <b>bold</b>, replace the TARGET word with ___
  - verb+noun → bold the verb, blank the noun: She had to <b>abandon</b> ___ when the visa was rejected.
  - adj+noun  → bold the adjective, blank the noun: We got caught in <b>heavy</b> ___ on the way here.
  - adverb+verb → bold the adverb, blank the verb: I <b>strongly</b> ___ seeing a doctor about this.
  - chunk → bold the key word, blank one word: Could you <b>say</b> ___ again? I didn't catch that.
- 3 sentences, genuinely different contexts — formal, informal, professional, spoken, etc.
- All 3 sentences must feel natural, not forced

Rules for BACK:
- Bold the full collocation with ** in the definition line
- Minimum 2 examples — different from the 3 front sentences
- Everything in English — no translations
- No numbering of cards, no extra commentary, just the cards

Words:
[YOUR WORDS HERE]
```
